# Recommended interview questions

This is a question bank for the interviewer, with reasoning. The interviewer doesn't ask all of these — it picks the right one for the moment, asks it, and follows the thread.

The questions are grouped by the three things we want to find out: **what people care about**, **what they want to know**, and **what they're worried about regarding the future**. There's a fourth group, **what they want from the collective**, that turned out to be load-bearing — most members already know what they care about; the harder question is what they want to *do about it together*.

The questions were chosen by reading the entire application_responses.csv and a sample of the Discord history. The patterns we saw:

- Most people gave very short, pat answers in the application form ("samuel asked me", "looks like a cult", "vibes"). The interesting answers came when they were given room to keep talking.
- The richest answers in the form (Georgios, Hoa, Leo) all came from open-ended prompts about *meaning* and *what's missing in the world*, not from "what would you like to create."
- A lot of members are at a "I want to do something but I don't know what" stage. Asking "what would you build" gets a shrug. Asking "what do you wish existed" gets pages.
- Almost no one in the application form mentioned what they're *worried* about. This is the biggest gap in what the collective currently knows about itself, and the strongest argument for doing this round of interviews.

## What you care about

These are the questions that get the conversation going. Start with one of them, not all of them.

1. **"What's animating you right now?"** — open and disarming. Doesn't require them to be at the meta level yet.
2. **"What were you doing the day you decided to join the collective? Do you remember why it felt like the right thing?"** — anchors them in their own decision and gets at motivation without asking "what's your motivation."
3. **"If you had a free year and money wasn't a constraint, what would you be doing?"** — the classic, but it works. Watch for the difference between what they say first and what they say second.
4. **"What do you wish more people in the collective were talking about?"** — surfaces what they care about by way of what's missing in the conversation.
5. **"What's something you believe that most of the people you know don't?"** — a way to get past the consensus answers without being adversarial. Variants: "what's the most idealist thing about you?", "what's the most cynical thing about you?"

## What you want to know

These are the questions that take some pressure off — they're permission to be confused.

6. **"What are you stuck on right now? Not as a problem to solve — just, what are you turning over in your head?"** — invites them to share open questions instead of finished thoughts.
7. **"If you could ask the collective one thing and get a real answer, what would it be?"** — gives the interview a use beyond information-gathering. The interviewer should follow up with a wiki search and try to actually answer.
8. **"What's a question you used to have that you've stopped asking? Why did you stop?"** — surfaces the questions that got dropped because they felt unanswerable, not because they got answered.
9. **"What do you want to learn this year that you don't know how to start learning?"** — pragmatic version of the above. Often produces a list the collective could help with.
10. **"Is there someone in the collective you wish you knew better?"** — useful for matchmaking and for the interviewer to learn social topology. Usually produces a name.

## What you're worried about regarding the future

The hardest section. Don't open with these. Don't open with "are you a doomer." Get specific.

11. **"When you imagine the world in ten years, what's the picture that comes to mind?"** — open. Lets them paint instead of label.
12. **"What's the thing you're most worried might happen that you don't talk about much?"** — the operative word is *don't talk about much*. This is for the worry that's underneath the politically-acceptable worry.
13. **"What worries you about the collective itself?"** — flagged as essential. Most members were extremely generous in the application form; very few said anything critical. The interviewer should explicitly invite criticism, more than once.
14. **"What's a worry you used to have that's gone away? What changed?"** — tracks the trajectory. Sometimes worries are gone because they were resolved; sometimes because the person stopped looking.
15. **"If AI goes the way you're afraid it might, what's the part you'll grieve most?"** — for members who care about AI, this gets past "I'm worried about safety" to what specifically they value that's at risk. Skip if they're not focused on AI.
16. **"Is there anything you've stopped trying to influence because it felt too big?"** — surfaces the giving-up-on, which is often more revealing than the still-fighting-for.

## What you want from the collective

This is the action-oriented section. Ask after the cares/worries questions, when the person has warmed up.

17. **"Concretely, what would you want to be different about your week if the collective was working well for you?"** — better than "what do you want from us." Forces them to imagine a specific change.
18. **"Is there a project on the site you wish you could join? Is there one you wish someone else would start?"** — practical. Pulls from `wiki/projects/`. The interviewer should be able to name projects back.
19. **"What do you have to offer that you don't think the collective knows about yet?"** — surfaces hidden skills and interests.
20. **"What would make you stop being part of this?"** — uncomfortable, important. The collective will lose people; better to know why in advance.

## On asking and listening

The interviewer should:

- **Open with one question, not five.** People shut down when the form has too many fields.
- **Use silence.** Most of the best answers come after the second silence, not the first.
- **Reflect, then ask again.** "What I heard you say is X — is that right? And if so, why does X matter to you?"
- **Quote them back.** "Earlier you said 'asymmetry is the engine of my love' — say more about that?"
- **Be willing to be wrong.** If the wiki contradicts something they say, treat their version as the new evidence, not the wiki as the source of truth. Add a note for the next lint pass.

## Questions the interviewer should *not* ask

- "Tell me about yourself." Useless. Always.
- "What are your strengths and weaknesses." This is a job interview question. This is not a job interview.
- "How would you describe the collective's mission." They don't owe you a definition of the thing they joined.
- "What are your goals." Too abstract. Ask what they're working on this week.
- "On a scale of 1 to 10..." No.

## Stage 1 — the pre-interview form

Before the conversation, the participant optionally fills a short form. These
answers are injected into the interviewer's opening context as a breadth map.
The interviewer must NOT re-ask these cold — they exist so it can go deep in
the right place instead of tunnelling into the first topic.

1. **what do you value about the collective?**
2. **where do you think we're falling short?**
3. **any ideas for things we could do differently, or things you wish existed?**
4. **would you like to get more involved? if so, what would you actually want to do?**
5. **newsletter — want one? how often, what would you want in it, what email?**

Plus a time question (quick-pick minutes, or "no fixed limit"). All optional.
Newsletter and time are captured here, not in the conversation.
